**Fig. 1 | Moscot fate probabilities track driver-gene expression, and moscot scales to one million cells.**
**a**, Entropy-regularized optimal transport on a toy example: four earlier and three later cells of generic cell types at positions *x*<sub>i</sub> and *y*<sub>j</sub> in a two-dimensional embedding, with areas proportional to their masses *a*<sub>i</sub> and *b*<sub>j</sub>. The coupling *P*<sup>\*</sup> (Sinkhorn's algorithm, ε = 2) sends mass from each earlier cell (row) to each later cell (column). *H*(*P*) = −Σ<sub>ij</sub> *P*<sub>ij</sub> log *P*<sub>ij</sub>; **1**, vector of ones. **b**, Simulated two-dimensional coordinates of 3,000 cells, colored by cell type (left) and Beta fate probability (right). **c**, Accuracy of predicted cell-type transitions per developmental stage; points denote n = 10 replicates per method and stage, jittered horizontally, with their median (horizontal line) and interquartile range (vertical line). **d**, Spearman correlation across cells between the expression of each driver gene and the fate probability toward its tissue, per tissue and method (n = 30 driver genes per tissue and method); methods colored as in **c**. Box plots show the median (center line) and interquartile range (box); whiskers extend to the most extreme values within 1.5× the interquartile range of the box; points denote individual genes, jittered horizontally. **e**, Runtime versus number of cells on logarithmic axes, one run per method and cell number. All data are simulated. Dim, dimension; WOT, Waddington-OT; TOME, Trajectories of Mammalian Embryogenesis.
